# Alternative Data for US Equity Prediction: Sentiment, Options Flow, Dark Pools, and Insider Signals

## Sentiment analysis: social media (Twitter/X, Reddit/WSB, StockTwits) and news — does it predict returns, and what's free?

### Takeaway
Retail tools/influencers market social sentiment as a leading indicator, but the credible academic evidence is mixed-to-weak for broad stock universes and shows strong evidence of **reverse causality** (price moves and volatility drive sentiment/volume at least as much as the other way around). Effects that do exist are short-horizon (same day to ~1-2 weeks), concentrated in high-attention "meme" names, and fade fast. Free data access is real but degraded: Reddit's API is no longer free at scale, Pushshift (the main free historical-Reddit research source) lost API access in 2023, and StockTwits' public API is frozen to new developers. News-sentiment APIs (Finnhub, Alpha Vantage) have usable free tiers but tight rate limits.

### Cited Findings
- Experimental evidence from a controlled online-forum study found a significant causal effect of social sentiment on **same-day** returns only — no significant effect on subsequent days — [ScienceDirect: causal relationship between social media sentiment and stock return](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0165176522001793)
- Research on the sentiment–volatility relationship found information spills over predominantly **from volatility to sentiment**, not the reverse, implying a large share of "investor sentiment" is actually a reaction to price moves already underway — [ScienceDirect: Spillover between investor sentiment and volatility](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1057521924005751)
- High stock volatility increases social-media discussion volume (uncertainty drives people to seek/share information), reinforcing the reverse-causality mechanism — same source as above.
- A Lund University thesis and related work on r/wallstreetbets found tone/volume had significant predictive associations with GME's intraday return, volatility, and put-call ratio specifically during the Jan 2021 squeeze — [IIM Bangalore working paper](https://www.iimb.ac.in/sites/default/files/2021-06/WP%20No.%20644.pdf)
- "I just like the stock" (peer-reviewed, *Financial Review* 2023) analyzed 10.8 million WSB comments and found the GameStop rally's sentiment impact was driven overwhelmingly by a small set of ~462 highly influential posters, not broad crowd sentiment — [Wiley: Long et al. 2023](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/fire.12328)
- A separate data-driven study of WSB found the forum provides "little to no signal" about future prices **except** for meme stocks (GME, AMC); Granger-causality tests showed WSB sentiment was not useful for forecasting returns on most stocks — [arXiv: Should You Take Investment Advice From WallStreetBets?](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2105.02728)
- One study covering Jan–Apr 2021 found no significant relationship between WSB sentiment/activity and respective stock returns in that sample — same arXiv paper above.
- A contrarian finding: higher individual-stock attention leads to more retail purchases, but sentiment itself behaves as a **contrarian** (not confirming) predictor of future returns — [arXiv: In the Red(dit): Social Media and Stock Prices](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2311.09252)
- Cross-asset/cross-source comparison: Google-search sentiment forecasts returns over a 3–7 day window, Bloomberg news sentiment over 7–14 days, and Bloomberg Twitter sentiment mainly over a single trading day — cited via [arXiv: Leveraging Social Media Sentiment for Predictive Algorithmic Trading](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.02089) (note: this is a trading-strategy paper, not a top academic journal; treat magnitude claims with caution).
- Foundational academic sentiment literature: Baker & Wurgler's composite sentiment index (closed-end fund discount, share turnover, IPO volume/first-day returns, dividend premium, equity issuance share) shows high sentiment predicts **lower** future returns, especially for small, young, volatile, unprofitable, hard-to-arbitrage stocks — [Baker & Wurgler, Journal of Finance 2006](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/j.1540-6261.2006.00885.x); a 2026 replication/extension exists ([Economic Inquiry, Leong](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/ecin.13290?af=R)) — note this is a market-level/cross-sectional sentiment index built from financial-market proxies, not social-media text, so it's a different (older, more rigorously validated) kind of "sentiment" than Twitter/Reddit NLP scores.
- Free data access reality: Reddit's API is free only for small-scale personal use; it becomes commercial (and billed) at $0.24 per 1,000 calls beyond free-tier limits (100 queries/min per OAuth client) — [Reddit API Pricing 2026](https://www.redditcommentscraper.com/article-reddit-api-pricing-alternative.html)
- Pushshift — the dataset most published Reddit-sentiment research actually used — lost broad API access after Reddit revoked it in mid-2023; public access is now unreliable/intermittent — [socialcrawl.dev: Reddit Data API 2026](https://www.socialcrawl.dev/blog/reddit-data-api-2026)
- StockTwits has a public API but it is "frozen to new developers" (no new API keys issued) — [7wdata: StockTwits API](https://7wdata.be/tool/stocktwits-api/); third-party scrapers/wrappers exist but add cost and ToS risk.
- Free news/sentiment API tiers: Alpha Vantage's News & Sentiment API covers 200,000+ tickers with AI-assigned per-article/per-ticker sentiment scores, but the free tier is capped at 25 requests/day; Finnhub (already in this project's stack) has a freemium tier with news + sentiment and higher throughput (~60 calls/min); NewsAPI.org's free tier is for development only with limited production use — [comparison summary citing Alpha Vantage, Finnhub, NewsAPI](https://www.alphavantage.co/) and [newsdata.io: Best Stock News API 2026](https://newsdata.io/blog/best-stock-news-api/)

### Inferences
- Because sentiment and short-term price/volume move together and volatility→sentiment causality dominates in several studies, a naive "sentiment score went up, buy" rule risks being a lagging/coincident indicator dressed up as predictive — it may just correlate with (not lead) the move the dashboard would already see in price/volume data from Yahoo Finance.
- Any genuine edge found in the literature is concentrated in a handful of high-attention, high-retail-ownership "meme" names and over very short (intraday to multi-day) horizons — not a generalizable factor across the broad market the way the insider cluster-buying factor appears to be.
- Given Pushshift's collapse and StockTwits' frozen API, a free-data sentiment pipeline today would likely have to be built on Reddit's official (rate-limited, ToS-bound) API plus Alpha Vantage/Finnhub news sentiment — meaningfully smaller and noisier than the data academic papers used.

### Gaps
- Could not find a single large-sample, multi-year, peer-reviewed study testing Reddit/StockTwits sentiment as a **standalone backtested factor** across the full US equity universe (most available studies are event-specific to GameStop/meme stocks or short samples); so the true base-rate predictive value outside meme-stock episodes is not well established either way.
- Did not find a rigorous independent (non-vendor, non-blog) comparison of finance-YouTuber sentiment claims against realized returns — this space is dominated by marketing content with no evaluated track record, so no verdict can be given beyond noting the absence of credible evidence.

## Options flow / unusual options activity: how it works, data access, and real evidence

### Takeaway
Unusual options activity (UOA) has genuine, published academic support as an information signal — but the effect is modest, concentrated in smaller/harder-to-arbitrage names, and the specific "follow what's publicized" version (CNBC-style call-outs, and by extension most retail flow-alert services) shows overreaction followed by reversal rather than real edge. Meaningful options-flow data (real-time OPRA-sourced trade-by-trade tape, broker/customer type classification) is fundamentally a paid-data domain; there is no free equivalent.

### Cited Findings
- Academic paper "Unusual Option Activity: Is it Smart to Follow 'Smart Money'?" (Jiang & Strong, SSRN) finds UOAs carry statistically and economically significant predictive power for subsequent stock returns, with stronger predictability for smaller stocks, stocks with higher idiosyncratic volatility, and stocks whose options' bid-ask spreads are wide relative to the stock's own spread — [SSRN abstract 3618427](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=3618427) (note: full paper was behind an access wall during this research; findings taken from search-result summaries of the abstract, not the full text — treat as a lead to verify, not a fully verified citation).
- The same research distinguishes a specific documented informed-trading context: ~25% of takeover targets show positive abnormal options volume beforehand, concentrated in short-dated, out-of-the-money calls — consistent with pre-announcement informed bullish positioning — [Wayne State news summary of the research](https://ilitchbusiness.wayne.edu/news/unusual-options-activity-revealed-as-a-potential-return-signal-in-new-finance-research-67679)
- Critically, for UOAs that get **publicized** (e.g., covered on CNBC), the research finds an immediate overreaction at the time of coverage followed by a subsequent price reversal — and trades covered on CNBC "are not very unusual" to begin with and do not produce abnormal returns — same SSRN paper, via [Institutional Investor summary](https://www.institutionalinvestor.com/article/b1mb4n52mrdsdr/is-it-worth-following-the-smart-money)
- General characterization from search-result synthesis: UOA shows "genuine predictive signal in some contexts, but the effect is generally modest, inconsistent across market conditions, and easily overwhelmed by false-signal sources" — this framing appeared in aggregated secondary summaries rather than a single named primary source; treat as directionally credible but not independently verified against the primary paper.
- Free vs. paid data: Unusual Whales' free tier is a 2-trading-day-delayed version of its platform (delayed flow, 15-min-delayed news, 30-min-delayed alerts) — essentially unusable for live decisions — [Unusual Whales free tier description](https://unusualwhales.com/)
- Real-time, continuously updating options-flow products are a paid category: Unusual Whales' paid plans start at $50/month ($42/month annual) for real-time options flow, dark pool data, and congressional-trading data; full API access with 100+ endpoints is a separate higher tier — [Unusual Whales pricing](https://unusualwhales.com/pricing)
- At the raw-data level, real-time full-market options quote/trade data (OPRA feed) has mandatory per-user licensing fees even for individual/non-professional retail use: approximately **$1.25/user/month** for non-professional real-time OPRA access, vs. **$31.50/user/month** for professional use; Cboe One Options Feed charges $0.60/month non-professional vs. $30.50/month professional, and direct exchange connectivity (e.g., OPRA via Cboe Connect) runs **$4,500/month** — [marketdata.app: OPRA Fees & Licensing](https://www.marketdata.app/education/options/opra-fees/), [Cboe BZX fee schedule](https://www.cboe.com/us/options/membership/fee_schedule/bzx/)
- Independent/critical commentary describes much of the retail options-flow-alert industry skeptically: one summary calls high-volume alert blasting (2,000+ alerts/day) "one of the most effective scams in retail trading today," with the claim that ~99% of the alert volume is low-information noise — [source aggregation, unverified primary attribution — treat as a strongly-worded secondary claim, not a confirmed statistic]
- Reddit-sourced trader discussion (anecdotal, not academic) repeatedly warns that flow tools alone are not reliably profitable and require independent confirmation — [tradeintelligent.substack.com](https://tradeintelligent.substack.com/p/anyone-else-sick-and-tired-of-all)
- A specific fraud case was documented: Optionflowstrade.pro was formally flagged as an unauthorized/unregistered entity by Spain's securities regulator (CNMV), with independent trust-scoring services rating it near zero — [Medium: Optionflowstrade.pro Scam](https://medium.com/coinmonks/the-optionflowstrade-pro-666c4c7537c3)

### Inferences
- The one well-documented, academically supported edge (pre-takeover OTM call buying ahead of M&A announcements) is a narrow, event-specific signal, not a general "follow unusual volume" strategy — and it requires detecting activity before it becomes public/unusual-looking to anyone else, which is hard without real-time data.
- The fact that *publicized* UOA (CNBC-style) shows reversal rather than continuation is a direct warning sign for any retail-facing "unusual options activity today" feed: by the time a signal is distributed widely enough for a retail dashboard to see it, the documented pattern in at least one peer-reviewed study is that the easy move has already happened or is about to reverse.
- There is no free tier of options-flow data that would be usable for a live signal; the free tiers that exist (Unusual Whales 2-day delay) are explicitly structured to be a marketing funnel toward the paid product, not a viable research/production data source.

### Gaps
- Could not access the full text of the Jiang & Strong paper (SSRN page returned 403); the specific abnormal-return magnitude and holding period for the general UOA effect (beyond the takeover-specific subset) could not be independently confirmed — this is a notable gap given it's the main academic anchor for this topic.
- Could not find a rigorous, methodologically independent (non-vendor, non-anecdotal-Reddit) backtest of a specific paid retail flow-alert product (e.g., Unusual Whales' own "smart money" or "dark pool" signal) against a buy-and-hold or random benchmark; evaluation of these products' real-world track record rests on marketing materials and informal community sentiment, not controlled studies. This should be flagged explicitly as absence of credible independent evidence, not evidence of absence.

## Dark pool / block-trade data: what's free, and what's the real evidence?

### Takeaway
Aggregate dark-pool (ATS) volume data is genuinely free via FINRA, but it is weekly and roughly two weeks delayed — useful only for slow, structural analysis, not for timing trades. Real-time, trade-by-trade, ticker-level "dark pool prints" (what paid tools like Unusual Whales sell) require a paid data feed; the evidence that this data has standalone predictive value is thin and mostly non-academic.

### Cited Findings
- FINRA publishes weekly ATS (alternative trading system / dark pool) volume reports for every security, free of charge, with roughly a two-week reporting delay — [Meridian: Free Dark Pool Data](https://meridianfin.io/knowledge/free-dark-pool-data)
- This FINRA data is a legitimate, legal, no-cost source — tracking it is explicitly legal for retail traders because it's a regulatory disclosure, and trading on public dark-pool data is standard practice, not insider trading — [darkpoolheatmap.com: How to Read Dark Pool Data](https://darkpoolheatmap.com/learn/how-to-read-dark-pool-data)
- A cited claim (attributed to "Tabb Group" research by one secondary source) states unusual dark-pool volume preceded significant price moves in roughly 60% of cases studied over a two-year sample — [pro.stockalarm.io / finwiz.io summaries](https://finwiz.io/market-structure/dark-pool-trading) — **this figure could not be traced to an original Tabb Group publication or verified independently; treat as an unverified secondary claim, not a confirmed statistic.**
- Even sources favorable to the signal caveat that dark-pool data is "a weight-of-evidence input, not a standalone trigger" and that no professional trader buys solely because of bullish dark-pool prints — same source as above.
- Real-time, ticker-level dark-pool print tracking (as opposed to FINRA's weekly aggregate) is bundled into the same paid products as options flow — e.g., Unusual Whales includes "dark pool data" starting at its $50/month tier — [Unusual Whales pricing](https://unusualwhales.com/pricing)

### Inferences
- The free FINRA ATS data is structurally more like a slow fundamental/positioning indicator (useful for a weekly or monthly research cadence) than a trading signal — it cannot answer "is there a big block trade happening in this stock today," which is what the dashboard's daily/intraday use case would actually want.
- The specific predictive statistics quoted by paid-tool marketing pages (e.g., the 60%-of-cases Tabb Group figure) could not be verified against a primary source in this research pass — this is a case where the research instructions' honesty requirement is especially important: this number should not be repeated in a final report as established fact.

### Gaps
- No independent academic study on dark-pool/block-trade data as a standalone predictive signal for individual retail-accessible strategies was found in this research pass (most rigorous market-microstructure literature on dark pools focuses on market quality/liquidity effects, not retail alpha generation, and was not surfaced by these searches — a deeper, dedicated search of market-microstructure journals would be needed to fill this gap).
- Could not verify whether any free, real-time (not weekly-aggregate) dark-pool print data exists anywhere; search results consistently point to paid products as the only source of near-real-time, ticker-specific block/dark-pool prints.

## Insider trading signals, going deeper: selling, insider role, cluster effects, and the academic consensus on strength/holding period

### Takeaway
The deeper academic literature strongly supports insider **buying** (not selling) as informative, strongest in small-cap, under-researched firms, over roughly a 12-month holding horizon — but the more important refinement (Cohen, Malloy & Pomorski 2012) shows that over half of all insider trades are "routine" (e.g., scheduled 10b5-1 sales) and carry zero predictive power; nearly all of the real signal is concentrated in identifiable "opportunistic" trades. OpenInsider.com is the well-known free retail tool implementing a version of this, and it already encodes some of the literature's role/cluster logic (filtering to open-market "P" purchases, flagging multi-insider clusters) for free.

### Cited Findings
- Lakonishok & Lee (2001, *Review of Financial Studies*), the benchmark study (NYSE/AMEX/Nasdaq, 1975–1995): insider **purchases** carry predictive information; insider **selling** does not — informativeness of insider activity comes almost entirely from buying — [LSV Asset Management hosted copy](https://www.lsvasset.com/pdf/research-papers/Insider-Trades-Informative.pdf)
- Firms with heavy insider buying beat firms with heavy insider selling by **7.8%** over the following 12 months; after controlling for size and book-to-market effects, this drops to **4.8%** — same source.
- Insider trading is most informative in **small firms** (confirmed across Seyhun 1986/1998, Pascutti 1996, and Lakonishok & Lee 2001); small-cap stocks bought by insiders earned roughly **7.4%** abnormal returns over the following 12 months (≈4.8% after size/book-to-market adjustment) — same source.
- Cohen, Malloy & Pomorski, "Decoding Inside Information" (*Journal of Finance*, June 2012, won the Chicago Quantitative Alliance Academic Paper Competition): more than half of all insider trades are "routine" (e.g., scheduled, recurring pattern) and are **not informative** about the firm's future; stripping these away isolates "opportunistic" trades that carry essentially all the predictive power — [NBER working paper version](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w16454/w16454.pdf)
- A value-weighted portfolio strategy that trades only on opportunistic insider trades earns abnormal returns of **82 basis points per month**, while the abnormal return from routine-trader trades is essentially **zero** — same source.
- The most informed opportunistic insiders are specifically **local, non-executive insiders** at geographically concentrated, poorly-governed firms — i.e., informativeness is not simply "higher rank = better signal"; a lower-level insider trading opportunistically (not on a schedule) at a smaller, closely-held firm can be more informative than a routine CEO transaction — same source.
- On insider **selling** specifically: later research finds "insider selling appears to have no predictive ability" in the influential Lakonishok & Lee framing, and stocks sold by insiders do not show negative abnormal returns in that study; other work finds insider sales carry a *statistically* significant but *economically small* negative signal mainly at the 1-month horizon, and when stock characteristics/performance patterns are controlled for, abnormal returns from insider selling become statistically insignificant — [search-result synthesis citing multiple papers, incl. InsideArbitrage academic summary](https://www.insidearbitrage.com/academic-research-related-to-insider-trading/) (note: the specific paper behind the "1-month significant" claim was not independently identified/confirmed in this pass — treat as a plausible but not fully source-verified claim).
- Practical explanation for why selling is weak: most large insider sales are routine — scheduled 10b5-1 plans or option exercises for personal liquidity/diversification/tax reasons unrelated to views on the company — consistent with the Cohen/Malloy/Pomorski routine-vs-opportunistic framing — [tikr.com: 5 Best Free Tools to Track Insider Trading](https://www.tikr.com/blog/5-best-free-tools-to-track-insider-trading-in-stocks) and [Financial6pack substack: Why Following Insiders Works](https://financial6pack.substack.com/p/issue-34-why-following-insiders-works)
- Cluster buying (the literature-standard definition cited in secondary sources: **3 or more insiders buying within a ~30-day window**) is treated as a cleaner, stronger signal than a single insider's purchase, attributed to the Lakonishok & Lee line of research — [marketpeel.com: The Insider Cluster Buying Signal Explained](https://marketpeel.com/blog/insider-cluster-buying-signal) (note: this specific "3 insiders / 30 days" threshold is a commonly cited retail-tool convention attributed to the academic literature, but this research pass did not confirm that exact threshold appears verbatim in a primary academic paper — flag as a likely-accurate but not fully primary-sourced detail).
- OpenInsider.com: a **free**, real-time (no login required) screener that ingests Form 4 filings as they hit SEC EDGAR, categorized by insider title, transaction type, price, and size; it displays how the stock performed 1 day / 1 week / 1 month / 6 months after each insider trade directly in the table, and supports filtering to open-market "P" purchases and spotting multi-insider clusters — [tradealgo.com: Insider Trading Signals / Form 4 guide](https://www.tradealgo.com/trading-guides/stocks/insider-trading-signals-how-to-track-sec-form-4-filings-for-investment-ideas) and [tikr.com free tools roundup](https://www.tikr.com/blog/5-best-free-tools-to-track-insider-trading-in-stocks)
- Newer, more recent academic framing: "Does Insider Trading Predict Market Returns? Evidence from Aggregate Opportunistic Insider Trades" (Huang, Lin & Zheng, SSRN, builds directly on the Cohen/Malloy/Pomorski routine/opportunistic distinction) specifically tests aggregate opportunistic insider trading as a market-return (not just stock-return) predictor — [SSRN abstract 4294492](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/Delivery.cfm/8e0fcf39-0c20-4f09-b463-cb2bab536638-MECA.pdf?abstractid=4294492&mirid=1) (full findings not retrieved in this pass; flagged as a promising follow-up paper, not yet confirmed in detail).

### Inferences
- The single most actionable academic refinement beyond basic cluster-buying is the **routine-vs-opportunistic** split: a Form-4-based factor could likely be meaningfully strengthened for free (using EDGAR's own filing data) by trying to flag/exclude routine/scheduled transactions (10b5-1 plans, option-exercise-and-hold patterns, insiders who trade on a predictable calendar) rather than treating every open-market Form 4 purchase as equally informative. This is a concrete, implementable-on-free-data next step that directly addresses "wasn't solid enough evidence yet" — the existing cluster-buying factor is probably being diluted by routine trades mixed into the sample.
- Given that insider rank is not simply proportional to informativeness (the most-informed opportunistic traders in Cohen/Malloy/Pomorski were local non-executives at smaller, poorly-governed firms, consistent with Lakonishok & Lee's small-firm result), a factor that *weights by seniority* (e.g., overweighting CEO buys) may be weighting the wrong dimension — firm size/governance opacity and trade "routineness" look more load-bearing in the literature than insider title per se.
- OpenInsider already represents a mature, free, retail-grade implementation of much of this literature (role filtering, cluster detection, forward-return tracking built into the UI) — it is a strong existing benchmark/reference implementation and a candidate free data source in its own right (via EDGAR, same underlying filings) rather than a competitor to replace.
- Insider selling is well-established in the literature as a weak-to-negligible signal on its own; building a "cluster-selling" mirror of the existing buying factor is unlikely to add much standalone value per the cited research, though it was not tested here as a true cluster-specific question (see gap below).

### Gaps
- Found clear evidence that insider selling in general is weak/non-predictive, but did **not** find a study specifically isolating "cluster selling" (multiple insiders selling together) as its own tested factor — it's unclear from this research whether clustering strengthens the selling signal the way it does for buying, or whether selling clusters are dominated by routine, company-wide events (e.g., coordinated post-earnings 10b5-1 executions) that would wash out any signal. This would need a dedicated search of the sell-side insider literature to resolve.
- Could not fully confirm the precise "3 insiders / 30 days" cluster-buying threshold against a primary academic source (vs. a retail-tool convention attributed to the literature) — the underlying 7.8%/4.8% buy-vs-sell spread and the 12-month horizon are solidly sourced to Lakonishok & Lee, but the exact clustering parameterization used by retail tools like OpenInsider may differ from any single academic paper's exact definition.
- Did not retrieve full text of the Huang/Lin/Zheng aggregate-opportunistic-trading paper; its specific findings on market-level (vs. stock-level) predictability remain unconfirmed.

## Across all approaches: what's realistically buildable on free data, and what fundamentally requires paid feeds?

### Takeaway
Insider-signal refinement (routine-vs-opportunistic filtering on top of the existing Form 4 factor) is the most promising free-data-only next step with real academic backing. News/social sentiment is buildable for free but on a degraded, rate-limited version of what the academic literature tested, with weak/contradictory general evidence. Options flow and real-time dark-pool/block data are fundamentally paid-data domains — free tiers exist only as deliberately-crippled marketing funnels (multi-day delays), and even the raw regulatory feed (OPRA) carries mandatory per-user licensing fees for real-time access.

### Cited Findings
- Insider trading (Form 4 / EDGAR): fully free, already the project's existing data source; the literature (Lakonishok & Lee; Cohen, Malloy & Pomorski) is primary-sourced to real academic journals (*Review of Financial Studies*, *Journal of Finance*) rather than retail blogs — see Insider section above for full citations.
- Sentiment: free-but-degraded. Reddit's official API free tier has rate limits (100 queries/min) and the once-standard academic data source (Pushshift) lost broad access in 2023; StockTwits' API is frozen to new developers; Alpha Vantage's free news-sentiment tier is capped at 25 requests/day; Finnhub (already in the stack) offers freemium news+sentiment at higher throughput — see Sentiment section above for citations.
- Options flow: fundamentally paid beyond toy/delayed access. Free tiers (e.g., Unusual Whales) are explicitly multi-day-delayed; real-time OPRA access carries mandatory exchange licensing fees even for non-professional individuals (~$1.25–$31.50/user/month depending on professional status, or $4,500/month for direct feed connectivity) — see Options Flow section above for citations.
- Dark pool / block trades: partially free (FINRA weekly ATS aggregate, ~2-week delay) but not free in any form that would support near-real-time, ticker-specific signals — see Dark Pool section above for citations.

### Inferences
- Of the four signal types researched, insider-trading refinement is the only one where (a) free data already fully supports it, (b) the academic evidence base is the strongest and most directly on point (Journal of Finance-level), and (c) there is a concrete, specific methodological upgrade (routine/opportunistic filtering) not yet implemented in the user's existing factor.
- Sentiment is "free" in name but the realistic free-tier data available today (rate-limited Reddit API, no Pushshift, frozen StockTwits, 25-req/day Alpha Vantage) is meaningfully thinner than what the cited academic studies used, and the studies themselves disagree on whether the raw signal is anything more than a coincident/lagging reflection of price action.
- Options flow and real-time dark-pool data should be considered out of scope for a free-data-only project; any version built without a paid feed would be using either worthless multi-day-delayed data or a FINRA-style weekly aggregate unsuited to the kind of day-to-day "unusual activity" signal retail marketing describes.

### Gaps
- None beyond those already listed per-section above; this synthesis section draws only on findings already cited elsewhere in this document.
