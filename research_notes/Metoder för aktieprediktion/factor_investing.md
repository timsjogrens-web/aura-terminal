# Factor Investing Beyond Momentum/Low-Vol/Reversal/52-Week-High

Scope note: this covers US equity factors NOT already tested (momentum, low-vol, momentum+low-vol composite, short-term reversal, 52-week-high proximity), with emphasis on free-data feasibility (Yahoo Finance price/volume history, Finnhub free-tier snapshot fundamentals, SEC EDGAR filings) and on replication/decay evidence.

## What are the other major academically-documented equity factors, and what data do they need?

### Takeaway
Beyond the five already tested, the academically "canonical" factor set is: value (HML), size (SMB), profitability/quality (RMW, QMJ), investment/asset growth (CMA), betting-against-beta/low-beta, accruals, net share issuance/buyback, payout yield, post-earnings-announcement drift (PEAD), insider-trading signals, short interest, and calendar/seasonality effects. Nearly all of the fundamentals-based ones (value, quality, investment, accruals, payout, PEAD) require historical point-in-time fundamentals that this project explicitly lacks for backtesting — the same blocker already noted for value/quality. A smaller set is computable from price/volume alone or from free, naturally point-in-time-stamped SEC filings (share-count changes, insider Form 4 buys, short interest, calendar effects, MAX/skewness-based price anomalies).

### Cited Findings
- The Fama-French five-factor model adds profitability (RMW, "Robust Minus Weak" operating profitability) and investment (CMA, "Conservative Minus Aggressive" asset growth) to market, size (SMB) and value (HML) — [Fama-French five-factor model overview](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fama%E2%80%93French_three-factor_model); model specified as market, SMB, HML, RMW, CMA — [ScienceDirect summary](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1062940820300942)
- Quality-Minus-Junk (QMJ, Asness/Frazzini/Pedersen) is built from profitability, growth, safety and payout/accrual proxies; long-short QMJ portfolios (1957–2016) earned significant risk-adjusted returns in all 24 countries tested — [QMJ paper](http://www.econ.yale.edu/~shiller/behfin/2013_04-10/asness-frazzini-pedersen.pdf); seven quality dimensions studied include accruals, bid-ask spread, balance-sheet liquidity, profitability, leverage, payout ratio, turnover — [NBIM discussion note](https://www.nbim.no/contentassets/0660d8c611f94980ab0d33930cb2534e/nbim_discussionnotes_3-15.pdf)
- Betting-Against-Beta (BAB, Frazzini & Pedersen, JFE 2014): long leveraged low-beta stocks, short high-beta stocks, produces significant risk-adjusted returns in US equities and 20 global markets, bonds, and futures; return compresses when funding constraints tighten — [BAB NBER paper](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w16601/w16601.pdf), [AQR](https://www.aqr.com/Insights/Research/Journal-Article/Betting-Against-Beta)
- Sloan (1996) accruals anomaly: low-accruals stocks outperform high-accruals stocks; this is distinct from pure low-beta/low-vol and requires balance sheet + cash flow statement data — [Quantpedia summary](https://quantpedia.com/strategies/accrual-anomaly)
- Net stock issuance anomaly: companies issuing shares subsequently underperform those buying back/not issuing; driven specifically by the issuance side, not repurchases; the international quintile spread between highest- and lowest-issuance markets is 0.85%/month — [ScienceDirect, share issuance international evidence](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0304405X09001007), [Monash research note](https://research.monash.edu/en/publications/net-stock-issuance-anomaly-and-cash-flow-explanation-a-research-n/)
- Payout yield (dividends + net buybacks) has materially stronger, more significant cross-sectional return predictability than dividend yield alone, which is often statistically insignificant on its own — [NBER payout yield paper](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w10651/revisions/w10651.rev0.pdf)
- PEAD (post-earnings-announcement drift, Bernard-Thomas style SUE sorts): classic PEAD based on standardized unexpected earnings has declined toward zero and is statistically indistinguishable from zero after 2017 in some studies, though text/NLP-based PEAD variants retain predictive power — [PEAD decline paper, Columbia](https://business.columbia.edu/sites/default/files-efs/imce-uploads/CEASA/Events%20Page/PEAD_Declined_over_time.pdf), [PEAD.txt](https://www.philadelphiafed.org/-/media/frbp/assets/working-papers/2021/wp21-07.pdf)
- Insider trading signals (Form 4): Lakonishok & Lee (2001) found heavy insider buying outperformed heavy insider selling by ~7.8% over 12 months (~4.8% after size/value adjustment), concentrated in smaller firms; "cluster buys" (3+ insiders buying within 15 days) show the most robust effect, especially officer purchases in small/mid-caps — [LSV insider trades paper](https://www.lsvasset.com/pdf/research-papers/Insider-Trades-Informative.pdf)
- Short interest anomaly: high relative short interest predicts negative abnormal returns because short sellers anticipate bad news/negative earnings surprises before they're public; effect described as potentially "transient" and of "debatable economic significance" in some samples — [NBER short interest paper](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w10434/w10434.pdf)
- MAX effect / idiosyncratic volatility / skewness: Bali, Cakici & Whitelaw (2011) — stocks with the highest max daily return in the past month underperform lowest-MAX stocks by up to 1.03%/month; the idiosyncratic volatility anomaly is largely subsumed by MAX in the US; this is a pure price-only factor requiring only daily return history — [MAX effect paper](https://pages.stern.nyu.edu/~rwhitela/papers/max%20jfe11.pdf)
- Seasonality: January effect is weak/arguably gone in US large caps but still detectable in micro-caps and some international markets; turn-of-month effect is more persistent than January effect across studies through roughly the 2000s–2010s but some recent samples show it absent; day-of-week effect has weakened substantially since the 1990s in liquid US large caps — [turn-of-month effect paper](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1057521903000073), [seasonal anomalies overview](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/247906180_Do_Seasonal_Anomalies_Still_Work)
- Value factor (HML) specifically has underperformed growth since 2007, with a ~55% drawdown through mid-2020; Arnott/Harvey/Kalesnik/Linnainmaa argue the decline is explained almost entirely by valuation-spread widening (not the model being "dead"), and an intangibles-adjusted book-to-price factor outperforms the classic HML by 1.3pp/yr overall and 2.2pp/yr post-2007 — [Reports of Value's Death](https://people.duke.edu/~charvey/Research/Published_Papers/P149_Reports_of_values.pdf)
- Size factor (SMB): the small-cap premium has effectively disappeared since the early 1980s in some analyses; over 2004–2023 small caps underperformed large caps by 1.2pp/yr; Asness et al. ("Size Matters, If You Control Your Junk") show the size premium reappears once junk/quality is controlled for, because small-cap indices are disproportionately loaded with distressed/illiquid "junk" firms — [size premium analysis](https://bradfordcornell.substack.com/p/whatever-happened-to-the-size-premium), [Size Matters paper](https://jacobslevycenter.wharton.upenn.edu/wp-content/uploads/2015/05/Size-Matters-if-You-Control-Your-Junk.pdf)

### Inferences
- Of all factors reviewed, only four categories are plausibly computable from this project's actual data stack without historical point-in-time fundamentals: (1) price/volume-only signals (MAX effect, idiosyncratic volatility, skewness-based sorts — all derivable from Yahoo daily/monthly price history); (2) calendar/seasonality effects (pure date-based, trivial from price history); (3) SEC-filing-derived signals that are naturally point-in-time because the filing date IS the information date — insider Form 4 transactions, shares-outstanding changes (net issuance/buyback) from 10-Q/10-K XBRL filings, and (where accessible) 13F-derived institutional ownership changes; (4) short interest, if a free historical source (FINRA bi-monthly data, or NASDAQ Trader's trailing-year feed) is integrated separately from Finnhub/Yahoo.
- Value, quality (QMJ), investment/CMA, accruals, payout yield, and classic SUE-based PEAD all require multi-year historical fundamentals snapshotted as of each historical filing date (not today's restated figures) — this is the same point-in-time fundamentals gap already identified as blocking value/quality testing, so these should likely remain out of scope unless a dedicated point-in-time fundamentals source is added.
- SEC EDGAR is actually a meaningfully better fundamentals source than "Finnhub's today-only snapshot" for a subset of signals, because EDGAR's XBRL company-facts API and raw filings carry a genuine historical, point-in-time-safe record (the filing date and the as-of date are both in the data) at no cost — this reframes the "no fundamentals data" constraint: it's not that no free fundamentals history exists, but that it exists only in raw SEC filings, not in a convenient current-only vendor feed, and extracting multi-year point-in-time fundamentals from EDGAR XBRL is itself a nontrivial engineering project (tag normalization, restatement handling, varying taxonomies across companies/years).

### Gaps
- No direct comparison found of Finnhub's free fundamentals (today-only) vs. what's extractable from SEC EDGAR XBRL specifically for small/micro-cap coverage quality or historical depth limits (XBRL mandate start dates vary by filer size, roughly 2009-2011 onward for most filers) — this bounds how far back a from-EDGAR value/quality backtest could even go, but exact earliest usable year per factor wasn't found.
- Did not find a current (2024-2026) up-to-date academic or practitioner study specifically re-testing the short interest anomaly's magnitude/significance in the most recent decade; the strongest sourced finding describes it as "transient" and of "debatable economic significance," which is already a caution sign, but currency of that specific claim is uncertain.

## Which factors are blocked by lack of point-in-time fundamentals, and which are feasible from free price/volume + SEC EDGAR filing data alone?

### Takeaway
Value, quality/QMJ, investment/CMA, accruals, and payout-yield factors are blocked for rigorous backtesting without a dedicated point-in-time fundamentals pipeline (the SEC EDGAR XBRL companyfacts API technically contains the needed history for free, but turning it into a clean, restatement-safe, point-in-time panel is a real engineering undertaking, not a simple API call). Feasible candidates using only Yahoo price/volume history and/or naturally point-in-time SEC filings are: MAX-effect/idiosyncratic-volatility/skewness sorts, calendar/seasonality effects, insider Form-4-based signals, net-share-issuance/buyback signals from XBRL share-count tags, and (with a supplementary free FINRA/NASDAQ data feed) short interest.

### Cited Findings
- SEC's data.sec.gov provides free, no-login, no-API-key JSON endpoints for company submissions and XBRL financial facts (companyfacts), and the SEC explicitly permits programmatic access — [SEC EDGAR API guide](https://dev.to/odeeb/the-sec-edgar-api-a-practical-guide-to-free-filing-data-in-python-15b)
- Balance-sheet XBRL concepts (e.g., Assets, StockholdersEquity, CommonStockSharesOutstanding) are tagged with a specific "as of" instant date rather than only a period, meaning balance-sheet-derived factors (e.g., net share issuance from shares-outstanding changes, or book value for a point-in-time book-to-price) are structurally point-in-time-safe if pulled directly from EDGAR rather than from a restated/current-only vendor snapshot — [XBRL US / SEC EDGAR data overview](https://xbrl.us/academic-repository/sec-edgar-data/)
- Finnhub's free tier covers insider transactions for North American companies (roughly 1 year of history per the docs reviewed), while institutional ownership (13F-derived) is a documented premium-only feature on Finnhub — [Finnhub free tier capabilities overview](https://github.com/RaquazaCode/Fintech-AI-SaaS-Using-Google-Antigravity-Skills-Stitch/blob/main/Finnhub%20API%20Free%20Tier%20Capabilities%20(2026).docx.md)
- FINRA publishes official bi-monthly short interest data for free (mid-month and month-end settlement dates); NASDAQ Trader provides roughly a trailing year of free short interest data by ticker; Cboe provides free short interest data for Cboe-listed securities — [FINRA short interest catalog](https://www.finra.org/finra-data/browse-catalog/equity-short-interest/data), [FINRA short interest explainer](https://www.strasmore.com/blog/finra-short-interest-data)
- Form 4 insider transactions are SEC filings, filed within days of the actual trade — meaning that unlike quarterly fundamentals, this signal is both free (EDGAR or Finnhub free tier) and naturally close to point-in-time by construction — [Form 4 tracking guide](https://filingtrack.com/blog/education-form-4-insider-transactions)
- The MAX effect (price-only) requires only the maximum daily return within the trailing month — fully computable from Yahoo Finance daily price history with no fundamentals at all — [MAX effect paper](https://pages.stern.nyu.edu/~rwhitela/papers/max%20jfe11.pdf)

### Inferences
- Insider Form 4 signals and net-share-issuance (from XBRL shares-outstanding tags) are the two most promising "new" factor categories for this dashboard: both are free, both are naturally or structurally point-in-time-safe, and both are conceptually distinct from the five factors already tested (they aren't repackaged momentum or low-vol).
- A rigorous value/quality/accruals/payout backtest remains blocked not by total absence of free data (EDGAR XBRL does contain multi-year point-in-time fundamentals) but by the data-engineering cost of building a clean point-in-time panel from heterogeneous XBRL tags across thousands of filers — worth flagging as a "blocked for now, not blocked forever" distinction rather than a hard wall.
- Short interest is feasible data-wise (free from FINRA/NASDAQ/Cboe) but requires integrating a second free data source beyond Finnhub/Yahoo, and the academic evidence on its current economic significance is mixed/aging.

### Gaps
- Could not confirm exactly how far back Finnhub's free insider-transactions endpoint goes (the source reviewed suggested roughly 1 year, but this wasn't independently verified against current Finnhub documentation) — matters for whether a meaningful backtest window (comparable to the 24-month holdout already used) is achievable purely from Finnhub, or whether EDGAR's own Form 4 archive (which goes back further) would need to be scraped directly instead.
- Did not verify the earliest year of full XBRL coverage across small/micro-cap filers specifically (large accelerated filers were mandated earlier, smaller reporting companies later, roughly phased in 2009-2011) — this bounds backtest length for any EDGAR-fundamentals-based factor.

## What does the "factor zoo"/replication-crisis literature say about which factors replicate vs. decay?

### Takeaway
The literature is split between a "markets are more efficient than reported" camp (Hou-Xue-Zhang: most anomalies fail to replicate under stricter methodology) and a "most anomalies do replicate, methodology choices explain the gap" camp (Chen & Zimmermann; Jensen, Kelly & Pedersen). Separately, McLean & Pontiff show factor returns shrink meaningfully after academic publication (consistent with either overfitting or publication-driven arbitrage), and Linnainmaa & Roberts show several canonical accounting-based anomalies — including profitability and investment, i.e., exactly the RMW/CMA factors — show no statistically reliable premium in a pre-1963 out-of-sample test, suggesting some of these "robust" factors may be sample-specific artifacts rather than persistent phenomena.

### Cited Findings
- Hou, Xue & Zhang ("Replicating Anomalies," 2020) replicated ~450 anomalies; roughly 65% fail to clear a t-stat of 1.96 once microcaps are properly handled (value-weighting, NYSE breakpoints); microcaps are ~3% of total market cap but ~60% of the number of listed stocks, making improper weighting a major source of spurious "anomalies" — [Replicating Anomalies research summary](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/345507035_Replicating_Anomalies), [factor zoo primer](https://skybluerw.github.io/2024/12/07/factor-zoo.html)
- Under Hou-Xue-Zhang's specific protocol, only ~36% of anomalies survive; but independent re-examinations by Chen & Zimmermann (2022) and Jensen, Kelly & Pedersen (2023) report ~98-100% reproduction of statistically significant anomalies, attributing the gap to differing measurement/methodology choices rather than the anomalies themselves being fake — [factor zoo primer](https://skybluerw.github.io/2024/12/07/factor-zoo.html), [Swedroe on factor zoo](https://www.etf.com/sections/index-investor-corner/swedroe-problems-factor-zoo?nopaging=1)
- McLean & Pontiff (JF 2016, Amundi Smith Breeden Prize winner): studied 97 return predictors; portfolio returns are 26% lower out-of-sample (same period, different sample) than in-sample, and 58% lower post-publication; they attribute roughly 32 percentage points of that decline specifically to publication-informed trading (i.e., investors reading the papers and arbitraging the effect away), separate from pure overfitting — [McLean & Pontiff](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/jofi.12365), [award summary](https://www.bc.edu/bc-web/schools/carroll-school/news/2017/pontiff-paper.html)
- McLean & Pontiff further find post-publication decline is larger for predictors with higher in-sample returns, and that return persistence is higher among stocks with high idiosyncratic risk and low liquidity (i.e., harder/costlier to arbitrage) — [McLean & Pontiff PDF](https://tevgeniou.github.io/EquityRiskFactors/bibliography/AcademicReviewFactor.pdf)
- Linnainmaa & Roberts ("The History of the Cross Section of Stock Returns," RFS 2018, Blume Prize winner): using hand-collected accounting data back to the early 20th century, most accounting-based anomalies show declining average returns and rising volatility when tested out-of-sample backward or forward in time; critically, profitability and investment factors (the FF5 additions, RMW/CMA) show no statistically reliable premium in the pre-1963 period — [Linnainmaa & Roberts paper](https://rodneywhitecenter.wharton.upenn.edu/wp-content/uploads/2014/04/12-16.Roberts.pdf), [NBER version](https://www.nber.org/papers/w22894)
- Accruals anomaly (Sloan 1996) specifically: multiple studies (Green, Hand & Soliman "Going, Going, Gone? The Apparent Demise of the Accruals Anomaly") find the hedge return decayed to roughly zero in the US by the 2000s, consistent with post-publication arbitrage, though Lev & Nissim (2006) found greater persistence — direct, documented example of exactly the decay pattern McLean & Pontiff describe generally, on one of the specific factors this report evaluates — [Going Going Gone paper](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=1501020), [Lev & Nissim persistence paper](http://www.columbia.edu/~dn75/The%20Persistence%20of%20the%20Accruals%20Anomaly.pdf)
- A 2023 Journal of Finance paper (Jensen, "Is There a Replication Crisis in Finance?") pushes back on the strongest "crisis" framing, arguing high reproduction rates are achievable with consistent methodology — [Jensen 2023, JoF](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/jofi.13249)

### Inferences
- The specific factors most directly implicated in genuine decay with strong sourcing are: accruals (clearly decayed post-2002, multiple independent studies), value/HML (clear post-2007 underperformance, though attributed to valuation-spread widening rather than fundamental breakdown), and classic SUE-based PEAD (declined toward zero post-2017 in at least one study). Quality/QMJ and BAB/low-beta have comparatively stronger "survives multiple re-examinations across 20+ countries and decades" evidence than accruals or classic PEAD — making QMJ and BAB the more credible "next things to test" from a pure replication-robustness standpoint, if point-in-time fundamentals access were ever solved for QMJ.
- Given this person has already run a rigorous locked 24-month holdout and found no edge for five well-documented factors, the Linnainmaa-Roberts finding — that even RMW/CMA (profitability, investment) lack a reliable premium outside their original 1963-forward discovery sample — is a reasonable prior to apply going in: expect most of the "next tier" of fundamentals-based factors to also plausibly fail a rigorous out-of-sample test, not because the testing methodology is wrong, but because a meaningful fraction of the published factor zoo may be sample-specific.
- The internal contradiction between Hou-Xue-Zhang (most anomalies fail) and Chen-Zimmermann/Jensen-Kelly-Pedersen (most anomalies reproduce) is itself an important finding to carry forward: it shows "does this factor replicate" is highly sensitive to methodological choices (value-weighting vs equal-weighting, NYSE vs all-exchange breakpoints, microcap handling) — which is directly actionable for this person's own methodology, since a free-data retail dashboard is very likely to be overweighting microcaps/low-liquidity names relative to how the original papers construct portfolios.

### Gaps
- Did not find the exact overlap between Hou-Xue-Zhang's ~450-anomaly replication set and the specific "beyond the big 5" factors in this report (e.g., whether short interest, net issuance, or MAX effect specifically were in their failing 65% or surviving 35%) — would need to read the full paper/appendix rather than secondary summaries to map this precisely.
- No source found giving a recent (2023-2026) re-test of QMJ or BAB specifically using a design comparable to this person's locked-holdout methodology (long-only free-data retail dashboard, no leverage, no shorting) — most QMJ/BAB evidence is from long-short institutional-style portfolios, which may not transfer cleanly to a long-only retail signal dashboard.

## How do people combine multiple factors, and is there good evidence combination beats single factors without just adding overfitting risk?

### Takeaway
There is solid evidence that combining a small number of genuinely diversifying factors (e.g., value + momentum, which AQR shows are strongly negatively correlated at roughly -0.6) improves risk-adjusted returns through diversification rather than through better prediction per se. In contrast, active factor timing (tactically rotating weight between factors based on predicted near-term performance) shows much weaker, often negative out-of-sample evidence and is explicitly flagged in the literature as prone to overfitting.

### Cited Findings
- AQR: applying style/factor strategies across multiple asset classes/signals simultaneously yields "substantial diversification benefits," with combined Sharpe ratios much higher than any single-style strategy; value and momentum specifically have roughly -0.6 correlation, making them "powerful diversifiers" of each other — [AQR Understanding Style Premia](https://images.aqr.com/-/media/AQR/Documents/Insights/Journal-Article/Understanding-Style-Premia.pdf)
- AQR's framework for combination is static (fixed) multi-style, multi-asset-class diversification — value, momentum, carry, defensive — risk-balanced across exposures, not tactical timing between them — [AQR Investing With Style](https://www.aqr.com/-/media/AQR/Documents/Insights/Journal-Article/JOIM-Investing-With-Style.pdf)
- "The Promises and Pitfalls of Factor Timing" (Jacobs Levy Center): explicitly frames factor timing as having both promise and significant pitfalls, consistent with a cautious consensus rather than clear positive evidence — [Promises and Pitfalls of Factor Timing](https://jacobslevycenter.wharton.upenn.edu/wp-content/uploads/2017/08/The-Promises-and-Pitfalls-of-Factor-Timing-2.pdf)
- A study on multifactor timing found that average out-of-sample R² turns negative once more than a single timing component/predictor is used, explicitly described as evidence that "factor timing is prone to overfitting" — [search synthesis citing multifactor timing literature](https://academic.oup.com/jfec/article/24/3/nbag006/8658726)
- Machine-learning-based multi-factor approaches show multitask/shared-structure learning across factors can act as a regularizer (reducing overfitting to any one factor's noise) relative to fitting each factor's timing model independently, but this requires economic restrictions to avoid overfitting given factors' low signal-to-noise ratio over realistically short samples — [factor timing with deep learning summary](https://academic.oup.com/jfec/article/24/3/nbag006/8658726)

### Inferences
- The strongest, best-evidenced form of "combination" for this person's context is static diversification across a few low/negatively-correlated factors (the AQR model) — not timing. Given the five factors already tested showed no edge individually, a combination/composite of them (beyond the already-tested momentum+low-vol composite) is unlikely to manufacture an edge from noise; true diversification benefit requires the underlying factors to actually work individually first, which these five did not.
- Active factor timing (overweighting whichever factor is predicted to do best soon) is the combination approach with the weakest and most overfitting-prone evidence base, and should be treated with the most skepticism for a personal dashboard — it is also the approach most likely to curve-fit nicely in-sample while failing a locked holdout, exactly the failure mode already guarded against.

### Gaps
- Did not find AQR or academic risk-parity-style factor-weighting evidence specifically scoped to a long-only, single-asset-class (US equities only), free-data retail context — virtually all strong combination evidence in the literature is from long-short, multi-asset, institutional implementations, which may not transfer to a long-only signal dashboard without leverage/shorting.

## Is anything in the factor-zoo literature specifically relevant to a free-data-only retail dashboard, as opposed to institutional factors needing expensive data?

### Takeaway
Yes: the Hou-Xue-Zhang finding that improper microcap weighting and NYSE-vs-all-exchange breakpoint choices explain a large share of "anomalies" that fail to replicate is directly relevant, because a free-data retail dashboard built on Yahoo/Finnhub/EDGAR with no fundamentals filtering is structurally likely to be overweighted toward small/illiquid/microcap names relative to how published factor papers construct their test portfolios — making naive replication of textbook factor returns on this data stack especially risky.

### Cited Findings
- Microcaps represent ~3% of total NYSE-AMEX-Nasdaq market cap but ~60% of the number of listed stocks; anomalies that aren't value-weighted or that use equal-weighted/all-exchange breakpoints are disproportionately driven by this small-cap/microcap segment — [factor zoo primer](https://skybluerw.github.io/2024/12/07/factor-zoo.html)
- McLean & Pontiff found predictors concentrated in high-idiosyncratic-risk, low-liquidity stocks show more return persistence post-publication (i.e., harder to arbitrage away) — but these same stocks are also the hardest to actually trade profitably after transaction costs/spreads, a cost not visible in paper backtests — [McLean & Pontiff PDF](https://tevgeniou.github.io/EquityRiskFactors/bibliography/AcademicReviewFactor.pdf)
- Asness et al.'s "Size Matters, If You Control Your Junk" shows the (disappeared) size premium reappears once junk/distressed firms are filtered out of the small-cap universe — directly relevant because a free-data dashboard without quality filters would include exactly this junk-heavy small-cap population — [Size Matters paper](https://jacobslevycenter.wharton.upenn.edu/wp-content/uploads/2015/05/Size-Matters-if-You-Control-Your-Junk.pdf)

### Inferences
- Any new factor tested on this dashboard should be checked for whether its apparent effect is concentrated in illiquid/microcap names before concluding it's real — this is the single most transferable lesson from the institutional factor-zoo literature to a free-data retail context, independent of which specific new factor is tried next.
- Because this dashboard has no paid liquidity/float filters either, it may be prudent to impose a simple price/volume-based liquidity floor (e.g., minimum dollar volume, minimum price) when testing any new price-only factor (MAX effect, idiosyncratic volatility, short interest, insider signals) — all of these concentrate in exactly the small/illiquid names where factor-zoo spuriousness is most common.

### Gaps
- Did not find retail-specific (as opposed to institutional, paid-data) literature discussing the factor zoo problem explicitly in the context of free-data dashboards — this inference is a reasonable extrapolation from the institutional literature's own stated mechanism (microcap/illiquidity weighting), not a direct citation.

## Overall Synthesis for Next Steps

**Feasible to test now (price/volume + naturally point-in-time SEC filings only):**
- MAX effect / idiosyncratic volatility / skewness sorts (pure Yahoo price history)
- Calendar/seasonality (turn-of-month more promising than January effect or day-of-week based on recency of evidence; pure date-based)
- Insider Form 4 cluster-buy signal (free via EDGAR or Finnhub free tier; naturally point-in-time; strongest documented effect in small/mid-caps)
- Net share issuance/buyback from XBRL shares-outstanding tags (free via EDGAR; point-in-time by construction)
- Short interest (requires adding a free FINRA/NASDAQ/Cboe feed alongside existing Finnhub/Yahoo stack; evidence is mixed/possibly "transient")

**Blocked without a dedicated point-in-time fundamentals pipeline (same issue as already-ruled-out value/quality):**
- Classic HML value, QMJ quality, CMA investment/asset-growth, Sloan accruals, payout yield, SUE-based PEAD. Note: SEC EDGAR XBRL companyfacts technically contains the historical data needed, but building a clean, restatement-safe, point-in-time panel from it is a nontrivial engineering project, not a quick API integration — worth flagging as "blocked for now" rather than "permanently blocked."

**Honest caution carried from the literature:**
- Linnainmaa & Roberts show even FF5's own profitability/investment factors lack a reliable premium outside their discovery sample. McLean & Pontiff show the broad pattern of post-publication decay (26% out-of-sample, 58% post-publication average decline across 97 predictors). Hou-Xue-Zhang/Chen-Zimmermann/Jensen-Kelly-Pedersen disagree sharply on how much of the "factor zoo" is real, with the resolution turning heavily on microcap/weighting methodology — a methodological sensitivity directly relevant to a free-data retail dashboard. Given this person's five prior rigorous tests already failed to find an edge, a reasonable prior going in is that most additional factors — especially the fundamentals-based ones once data access is solved — will also plausibly fail a locked holdout, and the price-only/SEC-filing-based factors above (MAX, insider clusters, net issuance) are the more tractable and better-evidenced candidates to try first, with explicit attention to liquidity filtering given the factor zoo's microcap-weighting lesson.
